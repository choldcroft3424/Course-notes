In a **metal**, the conduction band is half filled with electrons. The band gap is not a relevant quantity as metals naturally conduct.
- This means metals only need a small amount of energy to move an electron into the conduction band as there is no band gap (conduction band and valence band overlap)
- Metals can conduct near 0 K (absolute zero)

This means a metal can absorb energy, such as a photon, instantaneously. 
- The electron gains the energy of the photon ($E=h\nu$, $E$ is energy provided by a photon, $h$ is Planck's constant, the smallest unit of energy, and $\nu$ is the frequency of light). 
	- You can find the frequency of light with the equation $\nu=\frac{c}{\lambda}$, where $c$ is the speed of light and $\lambda$ is the wavelength.
	- Combine this with the first equation to get $E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$ which apparently is a much more useful equation
The electron then loses its energy as heat dissipation within a few femtoseconds.

In a semiconductor:
- An amount of energy is used to move electrons across the band gap into the conduction band.
- The electron loses energy as heat and moves down energy levels to the conduction band edge/minimum.
- The electron stays still in the conduction band for a few nanoseconds (interband transition) until moving back into the valence band

The important thing to remember is that semiconductors allow us to keep an electron in the conduction band for an extra nanosecond (which is a long time compared to femtoseconds).

An electron may emit a photon to move back into the conduction band, in this case the semiconductor can be used to make an LED.

Insulators have a "larger" band gap than semiconductors.

Promoting an electron to the conduction band creates a **hole** (lack of electron) in the valence band. This hole acts as a positively charged particle, so mathematically it is treated as such.

### Doping
*You don't need to remember the underlying chemistry in this section, but it is useful to know*

Silicon (Si) has 4 valence electrons, so each Si in a crystal has 4 neighbours:
![[Pasted image 20261006162559.png]]

Si forms covalent bonds with other Si atoms to reach a full valence shell (8 electrons) and become stable. (in the picture it's just the middle one, but this would happen for all Si atoms to infinity)

n-type doping is when the electronic structure is changed by replacing an atom.
![[Pasted image 20261006162807.png]]
In this case, phosphorous replaces Si, leaving one extra electron in the valence shell.

Energy from room temperature causes this electron to move to the conduction band and become delocalised.
![[Pasted image 20261006162859.png]]
The black bar indicates a dopant. The x axis is the position of the electron, the y axis is the electron's energy level.

This only needs to be done a few times to produce quite a lot of extra electrons, and thus quite a lot of conduction.
![[Pasted image 20261006163000.png]]
N-type dopants are 'donors' since they donate an extra electron to the conduction band. This is called N-type because it gives us negative charge carriers (electrons).

P-type doping can be done with a different atom such as boron, where instead of adding an extra electron, one is taken away:
![[Pasted image 20261006163154.png]]
This produces holes (positive charge carriers). This makes the material more insulative since it causes electrons to be moved to the valence band.

![[Pasted image 20261006163314.png]]

P-type dopants are called 'acceptors' since they accept electrons from the material.

**In essence, doping a semiconductor is engineering it to have an excess of electrons (n-type) or holes (p-type)**.

*These conduction band/valence band diagrams are not completely accurate, they tend to be drawn like this (and will be in future):*
![[Pasted image 20261006163634.png]]

The above diagram shows how a diode is made; an n-type semiconductor next to a p-type semiconductor. After some time, the electrons and holes will diffuse out and fill the space.
![[Pasted image 20261006164022.png]]

The electrons and holes meet in the middle. **Recombination** happens; the electron drops back into the valence band to fill the hole.
![[Pasted image 20261006164110.png]]
This leaves an empty region in the middle depleted of carriers, called the "depletion region". But we are still left with the original dopant atoms (phosphorous and boron) which leave fixed positive and negative charges. Now electrons become attracted to the positively charged side, and holes become attracted to the negatively charged side.

![[Pasted image 20261006164246.png]]
This changes the diagram as there is now an electric field in the middle. Electrons "roll down" like marbles towards the positive side, while holes "roll up" the bottom towards the negative side.
