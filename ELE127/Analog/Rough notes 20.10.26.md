Shining a light on a PN junction can excite electrons, moving them to the conduction band and creating more holes. 

Because of the electric field, this creates an additional current as the electron is moved towards the positively charged side and the holes move towards the negaitvely charged side.

![[Pasted image 20261022132007.png]]

The photocurrent is subtracted from the shockley diode equation, so at some voltages (around 0V-0.6V) the diode does not produce power, instead it extracts power (creating a solar panel).

You can extract maximum power at the point MPP (maximum power point) in the graph below![[Pasted image 20261022132722.png]]

The voltage and current at the MPP are $V_{max}$ and $I_{max}$

Look at the graph below:
![[Pasted image 20261022133007.png]]
When current = 0 (open current) we get a voltage of $V_{OC}$ since the photocurrent causes electrons and holes to move to opposite ends of the pn junction and cause a bit of charge to happen on either side.

When voltage is 0, there is a short circuit so current is at $I_{SC}$ (i.e. connecting the two terminals together).
