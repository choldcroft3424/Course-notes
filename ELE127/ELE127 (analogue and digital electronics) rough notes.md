## Analogue



Analogue and digital electronics will be taught concurrently. Analog electrical components include things like diodes, transistors and semiconductors.

A semiconductor is a material whose conductivity changes based on its environment (e.g. silicon is an electrical insulator at room temperature, and an electrical conductor at high temperatures.)

Semiconductor materials such as silicon form covalent bonds between their atoms, meaning electrons cannot freely move around without adding at least some amount of energy to the material. This amount of energy is dictated by **the band gap.**

An **electron volt** (eV) is a unit of energy (often used to replace joules in ELE127) which is equal to the kinetic energy gained by an electron when it accelerates across a potential difference of 1 volt in a vacuum. (1 eV = 1.6 x 10$^{-19}$J)

Electrons in the valence shell (or valence band, since it encompasses a group of energy levels) can receive energy (e.g. from light), moving it from the valence band to the conduction band. The energy needed to do this is the band gap.

Electrons in the conduction band are delocalised. The more electrons in the conduction band, the more of an electrical conductor the material is:
- The band gap in an insulator is large, so few electrons can enter the conduction band, so it doesn't conduct current.
- The band gap in a semiconductor is small, so electrons in the valence band can move into the conduction band if they receive external energy (e.g. when heating silicon) to turn the material into a conductor
- There is no band gap in a conductor (the conduction band and valence bands overlap), so electrons can move into the conduction band without receiving any energy, so the material conducts current.
